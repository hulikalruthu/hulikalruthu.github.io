Touch detection: asking Qwen where people touch

Qwen’s response (verbatim)
What is happening

A man and a woman are posing together for a photo, smiling at the camera. The man has his arm around the woman's shoulder, and they are both leaning in close to each other.

Where the touch is

The man's right hand is on the woman's left shoulder, and his left arm is wrapped around her back.

Box around the contact

[0.12, 0.32, 0.94, 0.61] as fractions of the image width and height; this is the highlighted area.

Teaching Qwen to find touch with GRPO

GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization): Qwen tries several answers, we score them against a human-marked touch area, and it learns from which answers beat the group.

Illustration of the idea: the boxes and scores are simulated, not our trained results. Photo: COCO val2017 #6763 via Flickr, CC BY-NC-SA 2.0.

Final output: a segmentation of the touch

After training, the answer is a mask instead of a box: the exact pixels of the two people’s hands in contact. Illustration: the masks were made with SAM prompted at the hands, not by a GRPO-trained model. Photo: COCO val2017 #6763 via Flickr, CC BY-NC-SA 2.0.

What’s hard for AI to do?

  • Tensing and going limp
    Subtle changes in muscle tone, with little visible motion
  • Speed of approaching someone
    • Different frame rates: 24, 25, 30 or 60 fps, variable rates and dropped frames change how far someone moves between frames
    • No depth from one camera: pixel motion is not metres per second, and the same speed looks faster up close than far away
    • Moving cameras: body-cam and shaky-cam motion mixes with the person’s own motion
    • Walking straight at the camera: it shows up only as growing size, which is the hardest motion to read
    • Motion blur, rolling shutter and lens distortion: positions smear and bend
    • Noisy tracking: small people, occlusions and identity switches make positions jitter, and speed amplifies the jitter
    • Edited or sped-up video: timestamps, cuts and time-lapse can't be trusted
